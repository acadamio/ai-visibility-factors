## What is it?

Third-party reputation platforms host customer reviews or ratings outside the business's own website. Depending on the market, examples include G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Google Business Profiles, or a specialist industry platform. Their value is that people can inspect experiences and concerns from other users, not simply a score displayed in isolation. Reviews need context: product version, reviewer type, date, sample size, and whether the platform allows the business to influence collection or moderation. This factor differs from testimonials selected and published on the organization's own site.

## Impact details

**Impact: Medium, particularly for local or purchase decisions.** Google says review count and positive ratings can help local Search ranking, and Business Profiles may show reviews from other local sites. Independent review pages can also appear as sources for a user comparing providers. That creates a plausible path to AI answers when the query asks about reputation or real customer experience. It does not establish that a higher star average on every platform causes AI recommendations. The source, question, and substance of reviews matter, and a rating alone may conceal serious limitations. [Google's local-ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en), [review-score guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4801187?hl=en), and [AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

## Proof & consensus details

**Proof: Medium. Consensus: Mixed** for AI visibility. Google's local Search documentation establishes a concrete use for reviews; it does not generalize that effect to all AI systems or all review platforms. Google's reviews system for editorial review articles explicitly does not evaluate user reviews in a product page's review section, which is a different mechanism. The public sources do not provide a cross-platform formula for how AI assistants weigh G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, or similar sites. Be careful with self-serving markup: Google does not show review rich results for an organization marking up reviews about itself. [Google's local-ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en), [reviews-system documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/reviews-system), and [review-snippet policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet).

## Recommendation

Identify the independent platforms that real customers use in the category. Make sure each profile identifies the correct product or location and links to current official information. Invite honest feedback through permitted methods, never buy or fabricate reviews, and respond constructively to recurring problems. Read review text and dates rather than optimizing solely for a high average. Track issues that require a product or service fix. When summarizing ratings on your own site, state the platform, count, and date so readers know what the figure represents.

## AI platforms

**Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** may surface Search-eligible review sources; Google separately documents a local-ranking role for Google reviews. [Google's AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

**Bing and Copilot** can retrieve relevant independent reviews, but their public guidelines do not assign a universal star-rating weight for AI citations. [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)

**ChatGPT** publishes no such weighting either. Evaluate whether actual AI answers cite the right platform and distinguish current customer evidence from old or unverified claims. [OpenAI's publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

**Claude:** Anthropic names reviews across multiple sources as inputs for product comparisons. This establishes a review-research use case, not a preferred review platform or star-rating weight. Check the product version and reviewer context in any cited assessment. [Claude web search examples](https://claude.com/blog/web-search).

**Perplexity:** Its shopping FAQ describes analyzing reviews across sites and using product details including reviews. This supports the relevance of third-party customer evidence in shopping, without establishing a universal review-count threshold or recommendation guarantee. [Perplexity’s shopping FAQ](https://hub-prod.perplexity.ai/hub/faq/what-is-shop-like-a-pro).

**Gemini:** Google Maps integration can provide place ratings. That gives local reputation data a documented route into Gemini, but does not establish a star-rating weight for recommendations or equivalent treatment of all review platforms. [Gemini’s Google Maps guidance](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16622866?hl=en).

## Audit instructions

1. List the reputation sites customers actually consult and find the organization's current profiles.
2. Check identity, product or location, rating count, review dates, recent themes, and any incorrect or duplicate profile.
3. Correct profile facts and address repeated customer issues. Test relevant AI comparison questions separately for accurate use of review evidence.
