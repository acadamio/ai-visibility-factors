## What is it?

Comparison content explains how two or more products, services, approaches, or alternatives differ on criteria that matter to a real decision. A useful comparison identifies who each option suits, what evidence supports each claim, and any meaningful trade-offs. It may use a table, prose, or both. A list of feature names without context or an unfair comparison against an outdated competitor is weak. This factor concerns the substance of the comparison; table formatting is covered separately under **Lists and tables**.

## Impact details

**Impact: Medium.** Many user questions ask which option is better for a particular situation. Google recommends explaining what distinguishes a reviewed item, considering comparable alternatives, and saying which may be best for particular uses. Bing asks pages to fully satisfy intent and value original analysis. A fair comparison can therefore provide the criteria and evidence needed for a grounded recommendation. It does not guarantee that an AI system will mention the publisher or favor its own product. The effect depends on the query, the quality of evidence, and whether the alternatives remain current. [Google's review guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews), [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a), and [Google's AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

## Proof & consensus details

**Proof: Medium. Consensus: Mixed** for AI citation impact. Google's review guidance strongly supports comparisons for helping people choose; Bing supports useful, original analysis. Neither provides a controlled estimate of AI citations gained by adding a comparison page, and the evidence is more specific to review and decision queries than to all topics. A promotional comparison may also lack independence. Disclose the publisher's role, use the same criteria for every option, and substantiate assertions about competitors. Treat recommendations as conclusions that must follow from evidence, not as labels attached to a preferred product. [Google's review guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews) and [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a).

## Recommendation

Start with the user's choice and select criteria that affect it: cost, capabilities, limitations, implementation effort, support, or fit for a use case. State the version, region, date, and pricing basis being compared. Use direct testing or primary documentation for factual claims and link to the evidence. Explain both strengths and drawbacks for each alternative, including when the publisher's own offering is a poor fit. Keep a concise conclusion tied to the criteria. Update the page when products or terms change; otherwise an apparently helpful comparison can quickly become misleading.

## AI platforms

**Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** can surface Search-eligible reviews and comparisons that help a user decide, but Google provides no comparison-format shortcut [Google's AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

**Bing and Copilot** favor focused, original, independently understandable information [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a).

**ChatGPT** publishes no rule awarding citations to comparison tables. When checking answers, look for balanced criteria and correct conditions, not merely whether the publisher is named [OpenAI's publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq).

**Claude:** Anthropic explicitly describes comparing product features, prices, and reviews across sources as a web-search use case. This makes accurate comparison criteria relevant, without establishing a preference for publisher-authored comparison pages. [Claude web search examples](https://claude.com/blog/web-search).

**Perplexity:** Its shopping experience presents product comparisons and draws on merchant information. Compare like-for-like specifications and current offers; the documented feature does not show that publishing a comparison page guarantees its selection. [Perplexity’s shopping announcement](https://www.perplexity.ai/en-GB/hub/blog/shop-like-a-pro).

**Gemini:** Deep Research analyzes multiple sources and includes Google Search by default. For comparison tasks, checking that a report preserves each option’s conditions is a practical audit inference, not a documented comparison-page ranking rule. [Gemini Deep Research guidance](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111?hl=en).

## Audit instructions

1. Select important comparison pages and write down the decision they are supposed to help a reader make.
2. Check that each option is assessed on the same relevant criteria with current, sourced facts, meaningful drawbacks, and a clear use-case conclusion.
3. Correct unfair or outdated claims and ask a reader to choose using the revised page. Test representative AI comparison answers separately for accuracy and balance.
