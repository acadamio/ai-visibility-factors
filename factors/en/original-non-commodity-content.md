## What is it?

Original, non-commodity content contributes something a reader cannot get from a generic summary of widely available material. It may offer a tested finding, a distinctive explanation, first-hand observation, a useful comparison, or a carefully supported point of view. Originality does not require every fact to be new. A page can explain established knowledge in a uniquely useful way; conversely, merely paraphrasing another article or using novel wording around common facts adds little value. [Google's AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) and [helpful-content guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## Impact details

**Impact: High where distinct information serves a real need.** Google's 2026 AI guidance says unique, valuable content is likely to influence long-term presence in generative AI Search more than its other suggestions. It specifically contrasts first-hand perspectives with easily reproduced summaries. Bing asks for original, authoritative content and warns that scraped or lightly rewritten material without added value can lose eligibility. This makes originality a strong editorial priority. It does not prove that a unique angle alone will cause a citation: the material must also be relevant, accurate, accessible, and useful for the particular question. [Google's AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) and [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a).

## Proof & consensus details

**Proof: Medium. Consensus: Strong** for the value of original information and analysis. Google explicitly asks whether content provides original reporting, research, or analysis and substantial value beyond source material. Bing similarly discourages copied content without added value. These are clear platform principles, though neither provides an isolated, numeric AI citation effect for “originality.” It is also not a license to invent unsupported novelty. A familiar, accurate answer may be the best response to a simple fact question, while original evidence matters more for nuanced or underdocumented topics. [Google's helpful-content guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), and [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a).

## Recommendation

Before publishing, identify the page's specific contribution: what did the author observe, test, compare, analyze, or explain better than existing references? Put that contribution in the main content and support it with methods, examples, data, or reasoned argument as appropriate. Attribute borrowed facts and quotations. Remove derivative filler and avoid producing many near-identical pages that differ only in target keyword. If the page is a synthesis, make the synthesis genuinely useful by reconciling evidence or exposing trade-offs rather than merely restating sources. Update original claims when new evidence changes the conclusion.

## AI platforms

**Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** have explicit official guidance favoring valuable, non-commodity information within Search. [Google's AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

**Bing and Copilot** ask for original content and may restrict low-value republishing. [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)

**ChatGPT** does not publish an originality scoring formula. A unique, well-supported page can become a distinctive source for a relevant answer, but actual inclusion depends on retrieval and answer selection. [OpenAI's publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

## Audit instructions

1. Sample priority pages and write one sentence naming the specific insight, evidence, or practical explanation each contributes.
2. Compare those pages with a few existing sources on the same subject. Flag generic summaries, copied claims, and sections that add no useful analysis.
3. Add the publisher's genuine evidence or perspective, remove filler, and record what changed. Monitor citations separately rather than treating uniqueness as guaranteed visibility.
