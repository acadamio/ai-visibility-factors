## What is it?

Use-case content explains the situation in which an offering or approach is relevant: the user's problem, environment, constraints, and intended outcome. It connects features to practical needs. For example, a service page might explain which team size, data sensitivity, budget, or stage of work it suits. It should also say when the offering is a poor fit. This differs from a case study: a use case describes an applicable scenario, while a case study documents a specific real implementation.

## Impact details

**Impact: Medium.** Recommendation questions often contain a situation rather than a product name. A page that explicitly ties an offering to that situation can answer more precisely than a generic feature list. Google recommends explaining which products may be best for particular uses and what key design decisions mean to users. Bing asks content to fully satisfy intent and be clear enough to stand alone for grounding. These support a plausible connection between useful scenarios and recommendation eligibility. They do not show that inserting a “use cases” section will produce more AI mentions, especially if the scenarios are vague or unsupported. [Google's review guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews), [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a), and [Google's AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

## Proof & consensus details

**Proof: Medium. Consensus: Mixed** for AI-specific visibility. Google and Bing favor content that satisfies a real decision and explains fit. The direct citation effect of publishing use cases has not been isolated in the platform sources. Use cases are strongest when they supply concrete criteria: audience, prerequisites, constraints, benefits, and limits. A statement such as “works for every industry” gives little verifiable guidance and may mislead. A real case study can support a use-case claim, but one success story does not prove universal applicability. [Google's helpful-content guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [review guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews), and [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a).

## Recommendation

Describe each priority scenario in the customer's terms: starting problem, who is involved, relevant constraints, how the offering is used, and the expected outcome. Name prerequisites and exclusions so a reader can judge fit. Connect each claim to a real feature, service process, or documented result. Keep use cases distinct rather than publishing many near-identical pages with industry names swapped in. Include a route to pricing or scope details where the decision requires them. Review examples when the product changes.

## AI platforms

**Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** may retrieve relevant Search pages for situational questions; Google warns against pages for every small query variation. [Google's AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

**Bing and Copilot** value focused pages that satisfy intent. [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)

**ChatGPT** has no published use-case scoring rule. A useful audit asks whether an AI answer recommends an option for the correct scenario and preserves the stated limits, not merely whether the page appears as a link. [OpenAI's publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

**Perplexity:** Perplexity explicitly presents research for shopping and travel decisions, including comparisons of product specifications. Explaining which constraints an offering satisfies is a practical implication for those tasks, not evidence of a bonus for pages labeled “use cases.” [Perplexity’s shopping and travel research announcement](https://www.perplexity.ai/changelog/what-we-shipped-may-30th).

## Audit instructions

1. List the main situations in which customers consider the offering and the conditions that determine whether it fits.
2. Check each page for a clear problem, audience, approach, prerequisites, outcome, and limitations. Flag generic scenarios that could apply to any offering.
3. Add concrete details and remove unsupported claims. Test a few real scenario questions in AI tools and compare the recommendations with the page's stated conditions.
