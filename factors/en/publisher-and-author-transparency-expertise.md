## What is it?

Publisher and author transparency lets readers identify who produced content, who is responsible for it, and why those people or organizations are qualified to address the subject. This can include an accurate byline, author profile, organization page, contact information, editorial method, and relevant credentials or experience. Expertise is demonstrated through the work as well as the biography: a named author does not make an inaccurate article reliable. Not every routine page needs an individual byline, but the source of consequential advice should be clear. [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## Impact details

**Impact: Medium.** Knowing the source helps a person evaluate a claim and may help a search system understand the publisher and author behind it. Google explicitly asks whether content has clear sourcing, evidence of expertise, and bylines linked to useful author background. It recommends making authorship clear where readers expect it. Bing asks for original, authoritative content and verifiable facts in pages used for grounding. This supports transparency as part of trustworthy publishing. It does not establish a simple AI ranking boost for adding an author box or credential list. [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a).

## Proof & consensus details

**Proof: Medium. Consensus: Strong** for the editorial value of clear authorship and relevant expertise; direct AI citation effects are unquantified. Google explains that its quality-rater guidance helps assess whether systems produce useful results, while raters do not directly control rankings. That distinction matters: “E-E-A-T” is not a single field to fill or a score a site can claim for itself. A fake expert biography or irrelevant credential can mislead. An author page is most useful when it connects a real person with their work, experience, and responsibility for the content. [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [Article structured-data guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).

## Recommendation

Name the organization responsible for the site and provide a way to contact it. On articles, reviews, or advice where authorship matters, show the author or editorial team and link to a profile with relevant background. Explain who reviewed technical or high-stakes material and when, if applicable. State methods, sources, and conflicts of interest where they affect trust. Keep visible bylines consistent with any Article markup, and avoid assigning a prominent person as author when they did not create or meaningfully review the content. Let the content's accuracy and evidence demonstrate expertise.

## AI platforms

**Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** inherit Search quality principles, but Google does not publish an author-profile requirement for supporting links. [Google's AI feature guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

**Bing and Copilot** emphasize authoritative, verifiable pages for grounding. [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)

**ChatGPT** publishes access guidance but no author-bio scoring rule. An AI answer might cite a page without naming its author; assess citation and factual accuracy separately from whether authorship is transparent to readers. [OpenAI's publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

**Claude:** Anthropic describes adding source-quality heuristics to Research after observing weaker sources displace authoritative material. This supports attention to verifiable expertise, but does not establish a byline or biography requirement for citations. [Anthropic’s Research system explanation](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system).

## Audit instructions

1. Sample important articles and advice pages. Check whether a reader can identify the responsible publisher and, where expected, the author or reviewer.
2. Open the author and organization pages. Verify relevant experience, working contact paths, and consistency with visible bylines and any Article markup.
3. Correct missing or inaccurate attribution and add useful method or review information. Monitor AI citations separately; a biography alone does not prove visibility.
